Tax

How much does it cost to register a boat in Spain? Taxes and fees in 2026

VAT, transfer tax (ITP) and the 12% matriculation tax (IEDMT): what you pay when buying and registering a pleasure boat in Spain, when, and how much, with examples.

Updated 9 October 2026 · 4 min read · Global Nautica team

It is one of the questions we hear most often at our office in Estepona. The short answer: it depends on three things. Whether the boat is new or used, its length, and who will use it and where. With those three facts you can work out which taxes apply and how much they come to.

The three taxes that may apply

1. VAT: new boats or boats bought from a business

If you buy from a shipyard, a dealer or any business selling in the course of its trade, the sale carries VAT (21% in Spain), usually included in the price. VAT is also due when you import a boat from outside the EU that does not have EU VAT paid.

2. ITP: used boats bought from a private seller

When you buy a second-hand boat from a private individual, you pay the transfer tax (Impuesto sobre Transmisiones Patrimoniales, ITP) instead of VAT. The buyer pays it, and the rate is set by the buyer's Spanish region. In Andalusia, the rate for pleasure boats over 8 metres is 8%, and it is lower for shorter boats.

The taxable base is not necessarily the agreed price. Every year the Spanish Tax Agency publishes official average-value tables covering thousands of boat models, with depreciation by age. If the contract price is lower than the table value, the tax office can claim the difference.

In Andalusia, ITP is filed on form 621 within two months of the purchase.

3. IEDMT: first registration in Spain

The Special Tax on Certain Means of Transport (IEDMT), known as the "matriculation tax", is paid once, when the boat is first registered in Spain, or when a Spanish resident uses it in Spain even under a foreign flag.

  • Pleasure boats over 8 metres: 12% of the boat's value.
  • Boats of 8 metres or less: not subject to this tax.
  • Boats genuinely and exclusively used for charter: may be exempt, subject to conditions (see our guide Can I charter my boat in Spain?).
  • Jet skis follow their own rules and are taxed regardless of length.

If you buy a boat that is already registered in Spain, the IEDMT was paid at the time. A change of owner does not trigger it again.

Worked examples

Take a 10-metre boat with a tax value of €100,000 and a buyer resident in Andalusia:

ScenarioMain taxes
Used boat, already Spanish-registered, bought from a private sellerITP 8% → €8,000. No IEDMT.
Used boat bought in another EU country (VAT paid) to be registered in SpainNo VAT again. IEDMT 12% → €12,000, unless exempt. Other taxes may apply depending on how the purchase is structured.
New 7-metre boat bought from a Spanish dealer21% VAT included in the price. No IEDMT, as the boat is under 8 metres.

These are simplified examples: the actual tax value depends on the official tables and on each case.

Other costs to budget for

  • Harbour Master's office (Capitanía Marítima) fees for registration, change of ownership and documents.
  • Third-party liability insurance, compulsory for pleasure boats in Spain.
  • Inspections and certificates: if the ITB inspection is due soon, factor it into the price.
  • A pre-purchase survey, highly recommended for used boats (see our checklist for buying a used boat in Spain).
  • Fees for the agent handling the paperwork.
  • If you are not Spanish, you will normally need an NIE (foreigner identification number) to pay taxes and register the boat.

Costly mistakes we often see

  • Declaring a price below the real one or below the official tables. The tax office can reassess the value and claim the difference with surcharges and interest.
  • Buying without checking the VAT status. A boat without EU VAT paid can end up costing 21% more.
  • Missing the ITP deadline or delaying the change of ownership: besides surcharges, the boat stays in the seller's name.
  • Assuming a foreign flag avoids the IEDMT. If the user lives in Spain, it usually does not (see Living in Spain with a foreign-flagged boat).

Frequently asked questions

Who pays the ITP, the buyer or the seller?

The buyer, at the rate of the Spanish region where they live. If you are not resident in Spain, ask us how it applies to you.

Is the matriculation tax paid every time the boat is sold?

No. The IEDMT is paid on the first permanent registration in Spain. Later sales are subject to ITP (private sales) or VAT (sales by a business).

My boat is exactly 8 metres long. Does it pay IEDMT?

No. Only pleasure boats longer than 8 metres are subject to it.
