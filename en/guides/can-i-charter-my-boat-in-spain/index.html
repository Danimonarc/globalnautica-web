Can I charter my boat in Spain? List 6, List 7 and foreign-flag charter

The difference between List 6 and List 7, what you need to charter a boat legally in Spain, the 2023 temporary commercial-use option and the tax effects.

Updated 9 October 2026 · 3 min read · Global Nautica team

More and more owners ask whether they can charter their boat when they are not using it, to offset berthing and maintenance costs. You can, but not just any way. In Spain, the line between private and commercial use is drawn by the "list" the boat is registered on.

List 7: private use

List 7 is for pleasure boats used for sport or leisure without profit. The vast majority of private boats are on it. As a general rule, a List 7 boat cannot be chartered.

List 6: commercial use

List 6 is for pleasure boats operated for profit: bareboat or skippered charter, sailing schools and other paid activities. Being on List 6 means, among other things:

  • Safety and equipment requirements suited to commercial use.
  • Insurance that covers charter activity.
  • For skippered charter, a skipper with the required qualifications.
  • Registering the business and meeting its tax obligations (VAT on charter fees, income or corporate tax).
  • In Andalusian ports, the relevant commercial activity licence.

What changed: temporary commercial use (Royal Decree 186/2023)

Royal Decree 186/2023, which approved Spain's Maritime Navigation Regulation, introduced a long-requested option: a privately used boat can be put to commercial use temporarily, for up to three months, subject to the clearance requirements, without permanently moving to List 6. Before, switching from List 7 to List 6 was complex and never temporary.

It is an attractive option for owners who only want to charter in high season, but the safety, insurance and tax requirements still apply during that period.

Tax: the matriculation tax exemption

Boats genuinely and exclusively used for charter may be exempt from the matriculation tax (IEDMT). For boats over 8 metres, where the tax is 12%, that is a significant saving. But there are trade-offs:

  • The owner's private use of the boat is very limited and can put the exemption at risk.
  • If the boat stops being chartered or is sold for private use, the tax will normally become due.
  • The tax office checks that the charter activity is genuine.

What if my boat has a foreign flag?

Since the 2023 reform, foreign-flagged boats, including non-EU ones, can charter in Spain under the same obligations as EU boats. You need the corresponding authorisations and should review the boat's VAT and customs position: a boat under temporary admission cannot be chartered without losing that regime.

Frequently asked questions

Can friends chip in for costs when they use my boat?

If there is payment, it counts as commercial use. Doing it with a List 7 boat without meeting the requirements can lead to fines and may void your insurance.

Can I use my List 6 boat for my own holidays?

Within limits, but that private use can have tax consequences, especially if the boat benefits from the IEDMT exemption. It needs planning.

How long does it take to move a boat to List 6?

It depends on the boat and its paperwork: equipment may need upgrading, insurance arranged and the change filed with the Harbour Master's office.
