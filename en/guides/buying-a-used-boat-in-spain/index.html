Buying & selling

Buying a used boat in Spain: a checklist before you sign

The checks on paperwork, debts, taxes and condition that prevent nasty surprises when buying a second-hand boat in Spain, and the steps after the purchase.

Updated 9 October 2026 · 3 min read · Global Nautica team

A used boat can be a great buy or the start of a long list of problems. The difference is usually what you check before you sign. This is the checklist we use with our clients, many of whom are buying in Spain for the first time.

1. Before negotiating: the boat and its paperwork

  • Ownership: make sure the seller is the registered owner, or has authority to sell.
  • Charges: check there is no ship mortgage, seizure or other charge on the boat. This is confirmed with a certificate from the registry.
  • Valid documents: navigation licence, Certificate of Seaworthiness and the date of the next ITB technical inspection.
  • CE marking, for boats built or imported into the EU since it became mandatory.
  • Ship Station Licence (LEB) and MMSI number, if the boat has radio equipment.

2. The boat's tax position

This is where the biggest financial risk lies, because unpaid taxes "travel" with the boat.

  • VAT status: if the boat comes from outside the EU, or EU VAT paid cannot be proven, 21% may be due on import.
  • Matriculation tax (IEDMT): if the boat is already Spanish-registered, it was paid at the time. If it has a foreign flag and will be used by a Spanish resident, it will probably be due now (12% for boats over 8 metres).
  • Register list: if the boat was on List 6 (charter) and exempt from IEDMT for that reason, moving it to private use may make the tax payable.

3. Condition: survey and sea trial

  • Commission an independent pre-purchase survey: hull, structure, osmosis, engines, electrics, gas and safety equipment.
  • Do a sea trial and review engine hours and the maintenance history.
  • Use the report to negotiate the price, or make the purchase conditional on repairs.

We coordinate surveys through our technical office.

4. The bill of sale

  • Full details of buyer, seller and boat (registration, name, hull identification number and engines).
  • Price and payment method, using a traceable payment.
  • The seller's statement that the boat is free of charges and taxes are up to date.
  • Inventory of included equipment and the date and place of handover.
  • For the change of ownership, signatures usually need to be certified, for example by a notary.

5. After the purchase

  • Get your NIE (foreigner identification number) if you do not already have one: you will need it for taxes and registration.
  • Pay the transfer tax (ITP) (in Andalusia, on form 621 within two months) or, if you bought from a business, get a VAT invoice.
  • File the change of ownership with the Harbour Master's office.
  • Take out third-party liability insurance, compulsory in Spain.
  • Update the LEB and MMSI to the new owner.
  • Check that your boating licence is valid for that boat in Spain.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreigner buy and register a boat in Spain?

Yes. People of any nationality can register under the Spanish flag, with a representative and an address for notifications in Spain. You will normally need an NIE.

Should I keep the boat's foreign flag?

It depends on where you live and how you will use the boat. If you are resident in Spain, keeping a foreign flag does not avoid Spanish matriculation tax.

Is a survey worth it on a small boat?

The older the boat, the more worthwhile it is. A simpler inspection may be enough for small boats, but it always costs less than a hidden defect.
