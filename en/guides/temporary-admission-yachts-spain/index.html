Customs

Temporary admission for yachts in Spain: the 18-month rule and the new 12-mile renewal

Which yachts can cruise the EU without paying VAT, for how long, how Spain lets you renew by sailing beyond 12 nautical miles since 2025, and when you lose the regime.

Updated 9 October 2026 · 3 min read · Global Nautica team

Temporary admission lets a yacht from outside the European Union cruise EU waters without paying import duty or VAT. It is the reason so many British, American and other non-EU yachts spend seasons on the Costa del Sol. But the rules are strict, and getting them wrong can be very expensive.

Who qualifies?

As a general rule, all of the following must apply:

  • The yacht is registered outside the EU.
  • The owner is a person or company established outside the EU.
  • The yacht is used by someone established outside the EU, for private purposes only.

If any condition stops being met, for example because the owner becomes resident in Spain or the yacht is chartered, the regime no longer applies.

The time limit: 18 months

The yacht may remain in the EU customs territory for up to 18 months at a time. Before the period ends, it must leave the EU customs territory to start a new one. If it stays, it is treated as permanently imported, with VAT and possibly other taxes due.

What changed in 2025: renewal beyond 12 nautical miles

For years, "resetting" the 18 months meant taking the yacht to a non-EU port. From the Costa del Sol, that usually meant Gibraltar or Morocco. In April 2025, Spanish Customs changed its interpretation: exit can now be evidenced by sailing beyond 12 nautical miles offshore. Spain had already applied the same approach to the inward processing regime for repairs since 2021.

Evidence normally consists of chart-plotter photos showing the position and route, with the yacht's certificate of registry visible. Two important caveats:

  • This is Spanish Customs' practice. Other EU countries may still require a call at a non-EU port.
  • Keep good records: in an inspection, it is up to the owner to prove the yacht left.

When you lose temporary admission

  • The owner or user becomes resident in Spain or in another EU country.
  • The yacht is used commercially, for example for charter.
  • It is sold to an EU resident.
  • The 18 months are exceeded without leaving.

In those cases import VAT (21% in Spain) becomes due. If the user lives in Spain and the yacht is over 8 metres, the 12% matriculation tax may be due as well. The Spanish Tax Agency checks marinas and identifies yachts that have overstayed.

Practical tips

  • Keep a log of entries, exits and users.
  • Plan exits well before the 18 months run out.
  • If you will stay in Spain longer than planned, or are thinking of moving here, get advice first: there may be better options than waiting until the regime is lost.
  • For repairs or a refit, look at inward processing relief, which allows VAT-free work under certain conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Does sailing beyond 12 miles work in any EU country?

Not necessarily. The 12-mile approach is Spanish Customs' practice; other member states may require a non-EU port call.

I'm British and spend part of the year in Spain. Do I qualify?

It depends on your residence. If you are tax resident in Spain, temporary admission does not apply and you may also have to register the yacht in Spain. See Living in Spain with a foreign-flagged boat.

Can I charter the yacht while it is under temporary admission?

No. The regime is for private use only. Charter requires a different set-up, with its own taxes and authorisations.
