Tax

Living in Spain with a foreign-flagged boat: do you have to register it?

Why Spanish residents who use a foreign-flagged boat in Spain generally have to register it here or pay matriculation tax, what Brexit changed for British owners, and the options.

Updated 9 October 2026 · 3 min read · Global Nautica team

It is a very common situation on the Costa del Sol: an owner moves to Spain and keeps using their boat under their home country's flag. Many assume that because the boat is not registered here, nothing is owed to the Spanish tax office. As a general rule, that is not the case.

The general rule

Spanish excise law requires means of transport, including pleasure boats, to be permanently registered in Spain when they are used in Spain by people or companies resident in Spain, or with an establishment in Spain. In practice:

  • If the boat is over 8 metres, the 12% matriculation tax (IEDMT) is due on its value, even under a foreign flag.
  • If it is 8 metres or less, it is not subject to IEDMT.
  • If the person using the boat is not resident and has no establishment in Spain, there is no such obligation.

When are you resident?

For tax purposes, as a general rule you are resident in Spain if you spend more than 183 days a year here, or if your main economic or family interests are here, among other criteria. Residence does not depend on nationality or on owning a home: each case needs to be looked at individually.

Non-EU boats: VAT as well

If the boat does not have EU VAT paid (for example, a British boat after Brexit, or one bought outside the EU), it is usually under temporary admission. That regime requires the owner and user to be established outside the EU. Once you become resident in Spain, you lose it, and 21% import VAT may become due on top.

Typical cases

  • A British owner who has moved to the Costa del Sol with a UK-flagged boat: import VAT is likely to be due and, if the boat is over 8 metres, IEDMT too.
  • A Spanish resident with a boat flagged in another EU country (for example, bought in France): no VAT again, but IEDMT is usually due.
  • A boat owned by a foreign company and used by a Spanish resident: this does not avoid the obligation. The tax office looks at who actually uses the boat.

What are the risks of doing nothing?

The Spanish Tax Agency and Customs inspect marinas. If they find a boat in this situation, they can claim the tax with interest and open penalty proceedings. It is usually cheaper to put things in order voluntarily than to wait for an inspection.

What are the options?

  • Register the boat in Spain and pay IEDMT on its current value.
  • If the boat will be used for charter, check whether the IEDMT exemption for charter boats could apply (see Can I charter my boat in Spain?).
  • If your Spanish residence is recent or unclear, get the case reviewed before doing anything.
  • Consider selling or relocating the boat if keeping it here does not make sense.

Frequently asked questions

I only spend a few months a year in Spain. Does this affect me?

If you are not tax resident in Spain, in principle no. But it is worth checking your days of stay and the other residence criteria carefully, as this is where most problems arise.

Would re-flagging the boat solve the problem?

No. The obligation depends on who uses the boat and where, not on its flag.

How is the IEDMT calculated on a used boat?

On its market value. The Spanish Tax Agency publishes official tables with average values and depreciation by age.
